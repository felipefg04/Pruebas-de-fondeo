How much should you risk per trade on a prop firm challenge?

Risk too little and you run out of time; risk too much and the daily loss limit ends you in a normal losing streak. In between there's a point where your pass odds peak.

The short answer

  1. 2-step with a 5% daily lossIn the examples, 0.75% to 1% per trade worked best.
  2. 1-step with a 3% daily lossThe sweet spot drops to 0.5–0.75%: the daily limit is tighter.
  3. Simple ruleDaily loss ÷ risk per trade = losses in a row you survive in one day. Aim for 4–6.

Bold marks the best risk in each column. At 0.25% almost nobody passes: the account grows so slowly that time runs out. At 2% the strategy passes in a few days when it passes, but the daily loss limit knocks it out more than 4 times in 10. On the 1-step, with only a 3% daily loss, pass odds collapse from 1% risk up: three losses in a row already end the account.

The "pure luck" column is the same volatility with zero edge. At 1–1.5% risk, about one in four attempts passes by chance alone: passing once doesn't prove your strategy works.

These are simulations, not real results. They don't include commissions, slippage or intraday open P&L (with it, the real daily-loss risk is somewhat higher). If your account has no time limit, low risk passes more often, but it takes months.

FAQ

How much should I risk per trade?

In the 2-step examples, 0.75–1%. On a 1-step with a 3% daily loss, 0.5–0.75%. The exact number depends on your strategy: test it with your own numbers.

Does risking more make me pass faster?

Yes, but you pass less often. At 2% the 45% strategy takes about 16 days and passes 40%; at 0.75% it takes about 59 days and passes 77%.

Is there a simple rule?

Daily loss ÷ risk per trade = how many losses in a row you survive in a day. 4 to 6 worked best.
