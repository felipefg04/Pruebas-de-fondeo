Static vs. trailing drawdown: how much it changes your pass odds

Two challenges can have the same target and the same 10% limit and still not be equally hard. The difference is where the loss is measured from.

What each one means

  1. StaticThe floor never moves: with a 100 account and a 10% limit, you fail if you drop below 90, even after reaching 108.
  2. End-of-day trailingThe floor moves up when the day's closing balance makes a new high. Close at 105 and the floor becomes 95.
  3. Intraday trailingThe floor moves up even with unrealized gains. A trade that hits +3% and comes back to 0 has already raised your floor.

With a trailing drawdown, every gain "takes away" room below you. That's why a normal losing streak you would survive with a static drawdown can end the account right after a good run.

If your firm uses a trailing drawdown, it usually pays to risk a bit less than you would with a static one.

FAQ

What is a trailing drawdown?

A max loss limit that rises when the account makes a new high. With a 10% limit and the account at 105, the floor moves from 90 to 95.

Is it harder to pass with trailing?

Yes, with the same limit. In the example, at 1% risk, the pass rate drops from 76% to 67%.

Intraday or end-of-day: which is worse?

Intraday: it raises the floor with gains you haven't closed yet. Check which one your firm uses before paying.
