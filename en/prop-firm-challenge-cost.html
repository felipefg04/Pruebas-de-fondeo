How much does it really cost to pass a prop firm challenge?

The challenge fee is what one attempt costs. What getting funded costs is that fee times the number of attempts you'll need, and that depends on your odds of passing.

The short answer

  1. Expected attempts = 1 ÷ pass rateIf you pass 25% of the time, you need 4 attempts on average.
  2. Expected cost = fee ÷ pass rateA $100 challenge at 25% odds costs you $400 on average.
  3. Risk per trade changes the mathThe same strategy can cost twice as much just from risking 2% instead of 0.75–1%.

A simulated example

Three traders with the same volatility and different edges: 45% win rate with a 1.6R average win (clear edge, +0.17R per trade), 40% with 1.6R (thin edge, +0.04R) and "luck only" (same volatility, zero edge). With a clear edge at 0.75–1% risk, getting funded costs 1.3 challenges on average. At 2% the same strategy needs 2.5: almost double the money, only from position size.

With a thin edge, the cheapest case is about 2.8 attempts, and the chance of failing 3 in a row is 26%. With no edge, even the best case is almost 4 attempts and you fail 3 in a row 41% of the time. If you've failed several attempts, the problem may be the strategy rather than bad luck: measure it before paying for another one.

These are simulations, not real results. They assume each attempt is independent and traded the same way. They don't include discounts, fee refunds on passing, commissions, slippage or intraday open P&L. Passing also doesn't guarantee a payout: the funded account has its own rules.

FAQ

How many challenges does it take to get funded?

On average, 1 ÷ your pass rate. In the example: ~1.3 with a clear edge, ~2.8 with a thin edge, 4 or more with no edge.

What does a funded account really cost?

The fee ÷ your pass rate. $100 at 25% = $400 on average; at 75%, about $133.

How do I lower the cost of getting funded?

Have a measurable edge and a risk per trade that doesn't collide with the daily loss limit. In the example, 2% instead of 0.75% nearly doubles the cost. A discount lowers the price, not your odds.
